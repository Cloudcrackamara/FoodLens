Where to look

  1. Near the barcode. Most packs print the number just under or beside it.
  2. Close to the best-before date. On tins and bottles it is often on the base, lid or neck.
  3. On the back or side panel, next to the ingredients or the maker’s address.
  4. On a sachet, check the folded seal at the top or bottom.

What it looks like

Letters and digits joined by a hyphen, sometimes after “NAFDAC Reg. No.” or “Reg. No.”. For example:

A7-0412L01-2298L03-1187L

These are demo numbers. Type the number exactly as printed, including letters. If the print is curved or faint, tilt the pack towards the light or type it in.

What a batch number is

A batch (or LOT) number marks a group of packs made together. It is usually printed with the production and best-before dates, for example LOT 24B-117.

What adding it tells you

  • Listed for this product: the company has added this batch to FoodLens for the product the number belongs to.
  • Not listed: the company hasn’t added this batch. It may be new, or the number may have been copied onto another product. Compare the pack carefully.
  • No batch entered: you still get the register result for the number. The batch is optional.

Batch lists come from the companies themselves. FoodLens shows them as provided and doesn’t test products.

What a lookup can tell you

  • Whether the number is in the demo register, and whether that registration is active or expired.
  • Which product and company the register lists for the number, so you can compare it with the pack.
  • Whether a batch you enter is listed by the company.

What it can’t tell you

  • Whether the pack in your hand is the original. A real number can be printed on a copied pack.
  • Anything about what is inside. A lookup isn’t a lab test.
  • How the product was stored or transported before it reached the shelf.

If something about a pack worries you, you can report it anonymously from the result screen after a check. Reports are reviewed by FoodLens and stay unverified until then.
